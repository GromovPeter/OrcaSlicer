# Hotend thermal response

The `Calibration > Hotend thermal response` entry opens the loaded-calibration workflow. Its CSV import/analysis actions observe recorded sensor responses without sending printer commands. Starting a loaded recording is a separate explicit action that controls the verified calibration job; see [the loaded-calibration design](loaded-thermal-calibration.md). The CSV analyzer can propose printer target settings; profiles change only through explicit application and normal preset saving. Measured sensor response is distinct from adaptive target slew limits and from melt temperature.

Required CSV columns are `time_s`, `target_c`, `temperature_c`; optional context is `power`, `fan` (fractions from zero to one) and `flow_mm3_s`. Timestamps must strictly increase. Numeric parsing uses the classic locale and accepts UTF-8 BOM, blank lines and comment lines starting with `#`. Column order is flexible. Duplicate headers, missing required values, invalid numbers and invalid physical ranges fail explicitly.

Analysis splits the record at target changes. Steps smaller than 5 degrees and heater-off targets are not calibrated. The preceding five seconds must stay within one degree of the previous target. Progress crossings at 10% and 90% are linearly interpolated between samples. Their elapsed span divided by the temperature span gives seconds per degree; extrapolating that slope back to the old target estimates a nonnegative delay. This is a descriptive approximation within the measured range, not an identified universal hotend model.

Settling requires the remaining plateau to stay within one degree of target, with at least ten seconds of observed dwell. Maximum directional overshoot is retained. Gaps over 2.5 seconds, incomplete response, reversals above two degrees within the ramp, missing fan/flow context or changing fan/flow exclude the transition from the summary. Excluded transitions remain visible with reasons. Accepted heating and cooling rates are summarized separately using the slowest observed response. Repeated measurements under relevant extrusion loads are necessary before a response summary can inform a printing-speed constraint.

`scripts/record_hotend_response.py` is a separate read-only measurement helper. It uses Moonraker object-list/query GET requests, retains Klipper's monotonic event time, records missing fan values as missing, and never issues G-code. An optional constant flow is declared test context, not inferred or measured flow. It refuses existing output files and stops on network/API failures or clock reversal, preserving collected CSV. Credentials may be supplied through `MOONRAKER_API_KEY` and are not written to the log. The separate read-only helper requires its fan mapping to match the actual machine. The generated-file recording workflow discovers its supported fan objects as described in the loaded-calibration design.

The numerical analyzer is covered by `[ThermalResponse]`; the recorder's request contract and missing-data behavior are covered by its Python tests. Synthetic demo CSV is explicitly labeled and cannot establish a printer's measured response.

The recorder supports an output-pin fan through `--fan-field value`. Repeated `--require-pin NAME=VALUE` options add normalized pin channels to the CSV and stop acquisition if a channel is missing or differs by more than 0.02. These are acquisition checks, not printer control: stopping the recorder does not cancel a separate temperature program. Additional pin CSV columns are retained for review; their constancy is checked by the recorder rather than inferred by the GUI importer.

Loaded printing logs additionally retain stage, requested_flow_mm3_s and both auxiliary fan pins. They use independent per-stage analysis described in [loaded thermal calibration](loaded-thermal-calibration.md). Ordinary numeric CSV analysis is unchanged.

## Printer settings proposals

A proposal requires at least one accepted heating and cooling response under positive printing load. Loaded logs qualify commanded flow and all recorded fan channels per stage; ordinary logs require declared positive deposition flow. Missing context, stationary experiments and one-sided/incomplete logs do not enable application. Excluded transitions remain visible and do not contribute. Results outside the existing printer setting ranges are unavailable rather than clamped to a faster value.

Measured 10–90% slopes remain separate from commanded target slew settings. For each accepted step, the provisional target time per degree is 1.25 times the larger of its sensor slope and (settling time minus extrapolated delay) divided by temperature change. Heating/cooling each retain the slowest resulting reference and round upwards to a tenth. Additional lead retains the largest observed delay plus one maximum observed valid acquisition interval, also rounded upwards. This accounts for a target change being observed at the next sample rather than timestamped at transmission. The 25% margin is a planning policy, not an identified physical model or a guarantee of smooth-ramp tracking, melt temperature or surface finish.

The dialog shows source CSV, measurement conditions, selected printer, current values and proposals. A 5-second flow-smoothing recommendation is optional and unchecked by default; temperature steps cannot identify this preference. Applying generates only three printer keys (rise, fall, additional lead), or four when smoothing is selected. It neither enables adaptation nor changes filament flow/temperature tables, material temperatures or motion. No profile format or default behavior changes.

Application edits the current printer through the normal tab/configuration path, retaining unrelated unsaved edits. Saving uses Orca's existing preset dialog and user-copy rules for system presets; it also saves any other pending printer edits, as stated in the dialog. Undo restores only the affected keys, retaining unrelated edits. After a save, undo produces unsaved restored values that must be saved again to persist. Undo state exists while the analysis dialog remains open. Printer identity is checked before application, saving and undo; a newly named user copy created by the normal save operation becomes the tracked target. A scrollable body keeps all controls accessible on smaller screens.
